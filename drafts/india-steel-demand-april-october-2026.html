India’s finished steel consumption grew 7.5% through October, outpacing production growth

Proposed byline: IndustryWire Editorial Desk · Drafted 16 November 2026 · Not yet published

India’s finished steel consumption reached 99.3 million tonnes (Mt) in April–October 2026, 7.5% above the same period a year earlier, while finished steel production rose 3.9% to 95.5 Mt. The Ministry of Steel’s provisional figures also show India was a net importer by volume during the seven-month period.

Finished steel imports totalled 4.90 Mt, up 28.9% year on year; exports were 3.98 Mt, up 15.5%. The difference is 0.92 Mt using the rounded published figures, consistent with the ministry’s net-importer classification. Consumption exceeded domestic finished production by 3.8 Mt, also using the rounded figures.

Prices moved higher in the ministry’s city sample

The October average price for 10 mm TMT was ₹66,645 per tonne, 26.0% higher year on year. Hot-rolled coil (2 mm) averaged ₹77,620 per tonne, up 31.6%. The ministry says these prices include GST and are based on second-fortnight averages from Kolkata, Delhi, Mumbai and Chennai. They are a limited city and product sample, not a national transaction-price series for every grade or buyer.

What the gap does—and does not—tell us

Consumption growing faster than reported domestic production, alongside higher imports, is consistent with imports contributing to supply during the period. The aggregate figures do not identify why buyers chose imports, which product grades drove the change, whether domestic capacity was available, or how producers’ margins changed. Those explanations need company, product and trade-flow evidence beyond this summary.

The figures are provisional Joint Plant Committee data reported by the Ministry of Steel. Readers should treat them as a period snapshot. Later releases and underlying company disclosures may add detail or revise the picture.

What to watch next

For a fuller market view, monitor monthly consumption and production, product-specific import and export data, price movements across regions and grades, and whether the import position persists. One seven-month comparison is not by itself a forecast of steel demand or pricing.
