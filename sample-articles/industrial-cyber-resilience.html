Cyber resilience for connected industrial operations

Editorial demonstration — not published news.

This sample is provided to show The Sector Journal’s intended explanatory style. It is based on the public references listed below but has not completed independent editorial verification. It does not describe a current company event, proprietary research result or market forecast.

Sample prepared 16 November 2026 · No author byline assigned

Industrial cybersecurity is not only an IT problem. Production systems, controllers, sensors, remote access, engineering workstations and business networks can affect physical operations. Security controls must account for safety, uptime, legacy equipment and the need to keep essential processes running.

NIST’s Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 provides a non-prescriptive way to organise cybersecurity outcomes. Its six functions are Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. NIST says the framework applies across technology environments, including operational technology. It is a risk-management structure rather than a step-by-step industrial control standard.

Start with an operational inventory

Identify critical processes and the systems that support them. Record owners, network paths, external connections, dependencies, software versions and recovery requirements. Prioritise the systems whose loss could create safety, environmental, production or customer impacts.

Make response plans executable

Define who can isolate an affected segment, who decides whether to shut down or continue operating, how engineering and IT teams coordinate, and how suppliers support recovery. Test backups and restoration procedures in a way that respects safety and production constraints.

Use a target profile

Compare current controls with the outcomes the organisation needs. Address remote access, identity, segmentation, patching, monitoring and incident exercises according to risk and operational context. Do not apply controls blindly if they can interrupt a validated process.

Sample analysis — not news. This general framework is not a security assessment or substitute for qualified OT security work.
