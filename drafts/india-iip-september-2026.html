India’s factory output grows 6.7% in September, led by intermediate and capital goods

Proposed byline: The Sector Journal Editorial Desk · Drafted 17 November 2026 · Not yet published

India’s industrial production rose 6.7% year on year in September, with output growth strongest in intermediate goods and capital goods, official estimates show. The use-based split was much weaker for consumer non-durables, which grew 1.4%.

The National Statistics Office’s quick estimate recorded 12.2% growth in the intermediate-goods index and 10.4% in capital goods, compared with 7.2% for infrastructure and construction goods, 6.0% for consumer durables and 1.4% for consumer non-durables. The pattern shows that September’s headline expansion was uneven across categories; it does not, by itself, establish the strength of end-consumer demand.

Manufacturing advanced as mining contracted

Manufacturing output grew 7.6% year on year and electricity and gas supply rose 11.2%, while mining and quarrying fell 6.2%. Seventeen of the 23 manufacturing industry groups registered positive growth. The ministry identified electrical equipment, rubber and plastics, and motor vehicles among the leading contributors.

The headline IIP combines these different sectors and categories. Its 6.7% growth rate is a volume indicator for industrial production, not a measure of company revenue, prices, orders, employment or profit. Those questions require other datasets and company evidence.

October output data are due later this month

The September figures are quick estimates and can be revised when updated factory data arrive. The release reports a weighted response rate of 86.5% for September. MoSPI’s release notice schedules the October IIP release for 30 November 2026, which should provide the next monthly reading.
