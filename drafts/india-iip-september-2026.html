India’s September factory-output growth: what the index does—and does not—show

Proposed byline: IndustryWire Editorial Desk · Drafted 16 November 2026 · Not yet published

India’s factory production index rose 7.6% year on year in September 2026, while the overall Index of Industrial Production (IIP) rose 6.7%. The figures point to growth in measured industrial output, but they do not show whether individual manufacturers’ sales, profits or order books improved.

The National Statistics Office’s quick estimate puts September’s manufacturing index at 128.0, compared with 119.0 a year earlier. Mining and quarrying declined 6.2% year on year, while electricity and gas supply grew 11.2%. The overall index was 124.3, up from 116.5 in September 2025.

Manufacturing growth was broad, but uneven

The release says 17 of 23 manufacturing industry groups recorded positive year-on-year growth. It identifies motor vehicles, electrical equipment, and rubber and plastics as the top positive contributors; the listed growth rates for those groups were 19.0%, 33.9% and 19.9%, respectively. The use-based figures also show faster growth in intermediate goods (12.2%) and capital goods (10.4%) than in consumer non-durables (1.4%). These categories point to variation within the headline, not a uniform rise across every factory or product.

The 6.7% headline combines different parts of industrial production. Manufacturing grew faster than the overall index, electricity and gas supply grew faster still, and mining contracted. A single aggregate therefore conceals divergent sector conditions; it should not be read as a uniform description of every industrial company.

What the IIP measures

The IIP is a short-term volume indicator for a basket of industrial products, compared with a chosen base period. The September release uses the 2022–23 base. It is not a direct measure of company revenue, prices, capacity utilisation, employment or profitability. Those questions need other data and, often, company-level evidence.

Why the estimate can change

The NSO labels the monthly figure a quick estimate and says it can be revised in later releases as source agencies provide updated factory data. The September estimate was compiled at a weighted response rate of 86.5%, according to the release. Readers should treat the number as an early official estimate, not a final, immutable reading. The year-on-year comparison also compares September with September a year earlier; it does not by itself describe month-to-month momentum.

What to watch next

For a fuller view, readers can compare later IIP revisions, use-based categories, and related indicators such as wholesale prices, trade and company disclosures. These measure different things and should not be combined as if they were interchangeable. This draft does not infer a cause for the September result or make a forecast.
