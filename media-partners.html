WORKING WITH INDUSTRY MEDIA

Media partnerships, built on clear boundaries.

IndustryWire is exploring how to collaborate with credible trade publications, professional associations, research groups, event organizers and specialist newsletters as the newsroom develops.

Current status

This is a partnership framework, not an announcement of existing partners. IndustryWire has no established syndication network, audience figures or guaranteed distribution service at this time.

Partnerships we may explore

Editorial collaboration

Co-reporting and expert access

Coordinate interviews, briefings or reporting on a clearly defined industry development while keeping each outlet’s editorial decisions independent.

Audience exchange

Events and specialist briefings

Share relevant public event information, speaker access or useful research briefings with appropriate attribution and no promise of coverage.

Future distribution

Excerpts, links or licensed content

Consider a limited content-sharing pilot only after agreeing written permissions, attribution, update responsibilities and how the original article will be identified.

How a partnership would work

  1. Propose a specific fit. Name the outlet or organization, audience, industry, geography and collaboration you have in mind.
  2. Check standards and rights. Agree who reports, edits, approves, owns and can republish each item. Verify image, data and quotation permissions.
  3. Run a small pilot. Start with one defined activity and a clear attribution link. Avoid copying full articles across sites until rights and search handling are agreed.
  4. Review the result. Discuss audience response and practical value before deciding whether to continue. No reach or search-ranking result is promised.

Editorial independence

A partnership would not buy favorable coverage, a guaranteed article, control over conclusions or a link in independent reporting. Any sponsored placement or company-supplied announcement would be labelled separately. Editorial collaboration and commercial publishing would have separate terms.

What to include in an enquiry

Tell us your organization and role, the audience and markets you serve, the proposed collaboration, relevant public examples and any deadlines. Please do not send confidential material, personal data about sources or attachments in an initial email.
