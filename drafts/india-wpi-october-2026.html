India’s wholesale inflation reached 12.29% in October as fuel pressures accelerated

Proposed byline: IndustryWire Editorial Desk · Drafted 16 November 2026 · Not yet published

India’s wholesale price inflation rose to 12.29% year on year in October 2026 from 11.39% in September, according to provisional government data released on 16 November. The sharpest change among the three major groups was in Fuel and Power, where the annual rate accelerated to 32.84% from 25.85%.

The provisional All Commodities Wholesale Price Index (WPI) increased to 113.3 in October from 112.5 in September. Primary Articles inflation was 9.24%, up from 8.67%. Manufactured Products inflation eased slightly to 9.54% from 9.76%.

Manufacturing’s rate edged down while the headline rose

The headline’s rise did not mean every group accelerated. The annual rate for manufactured products declined by 0.22 percentage points, while the Fuel and Power rate increased by 6.99 points. The release identifies mineral oils, crude petroleum and natural gas, electricity, food articles and several manufactured categories among the drivers of October wholesale inflation.

These are group-level price indicators, not company cost or profit measures. The data alone cannot show how a particular producer’s input costs changed, whether a company passed costs on to customers, or what happened to its margins.

Read the release as provisional

The October WPI is provisional and was compiled with a weighted response rate of 81.1%, according to the release. The index is due to be updated in the regular release cycle. The year-on-year rate compares October with October a year earlier; the monthly index comparison (113.3 versus 112.5) answers a different question and should not be described as a 12.29% monthly increase.

What the indicator can and cannot answer

WPI tracks wholesale price changes across a weighted basket. It is not the consumer price index and does not measure the retail prices paid by households. For industrial readers, it is one broad input to price context; company disclosures, commodity-specific data and contract terms are needed to understand sector or firm-level effects.
