# The Sector Journal client publishing terms — working draft

**Internal planning document. Not a published contract or legal advice.** India is the planned operating country, and Kavita is the named publisher, not the firm’s owner. The owner, legal operator structure, tax position, host, payment provider, publication workflow, target-market jurisdictions and any distribution partners are not yet confirmed. Have India-qualified counsel adapt and review this document for the actual business and any other intended jurisdictions before taking payment or accepting customer content.

## 1. Service and order acceptance

The Sector Journal may offer review and publication of eligible company announcements and attributed market-research report summaries. The exact item, format, price, applicable taxes, timing, included channels, customer responsibilities, edit allowance, and any removal terms must be shown in the plan or agreed in writing before purchase. An enquiry or upload alone does not mean the item has been accepted. The service begins only after The Sector Journal confirms the order and the required payment/credits are available.

The Sector Journal may decline, pause, edit for clarity, or remove material that is unsupported, misleading, unlawful, rights-unclear, unsuitable for the publication, or inconsistent with its editorial and safety rules. Paid client content remains labelled and separate from independent reporting. Payment does not buy independent newsroom coverage, a favourable conclusion, a search position, traffic, leads, sales, or platform inclusion.

## 2. Content, accuracy, and permissions

The customer is responsible for the accuracy and completeness of its supplied content and for the right to submit it. The customer must have permission for all text, images, logos, data, quotations, personal information, named individuals, and third-party materials it supplies. It must identify material claims that require attribution, substantiate factual claims when asked, and disclose any sponsorship or commercial interest that readers need to understand.

The customer must not submit unlawful, deceptive, defamatory, infringing, privacy-violating, unsafe, or materially misleading content. The Sector Journal may ask for corrections, evidence, attribution, or proof of permission; it may decline content that does not meet its requirements. The Sector Journal's editorial review is not a guarantee that every customer claim has been independently verified.

## 3. Proof, publication, and one edit per article

Before publication, the customer receives a proof to check names, dates, contact details, links, factual particulars, and permissions. The Sector Journal makes the final publication decision and keeps responsibility for the page's labels and editorial presentation.

After publication, the customer may request **one post-publication edit for that article**. The allowance is attached to the individual article, is not transferable, and cannot be pooled with unused allowances from other articles. For example, a five-article plan gives one edit on each of five published articles, not five edits on one article. The dashboard must show whether each article's one edit is unused or spent. The requested change is subject to review and may be declined if it would make the page misleading, unlawful, unsupported, or inconsistent with the agreed scope.

The one-edit limit does not prevent The Sector Journal from correcting its own factual errors, correcting disclosures, responding to legal requirements, or making editorial changes that it considers necessary. These actions are recorded separately from customer edit requests. The precise turnaround and meaning of a qualifying edit must be set before launch.

## 4. Plans, article credits, and dashboard

Each plan must state its price, number of article credits, validity period, renewal/cancellation conditions, applicable taxes, and whether unused credits expire or roll over. Do not publish plan pricing until these terms agree across the plan page, checkout, dashboard, and contract.

For a five-article plan, the system must prevent the customer from submitting or publishing a sixth article until renewal or a top-up supplies another valid credit. The dashboard should distinguish articles purchased, submitted/uploaded, in review, accepted, published, declined, and remaining. It must also show the one-edit allowance for each published article. Customers may access only their own organisation's accounts, content, billing records, and usage. The credit event (submission, acceptance, or publication) and treatment of declined, withdrawn, duplicate, or incomplete items must be selected and displayed before sales begin.

## 5. Distribution and third-party services

Unless the accepted order expressly says otherwise, the initial service is publication on The Sector Journal only. No RSS feed, email distribution, social posting, directory placement, partner syndication, placement on other The Sector Journal properties, or named external-platform placement is included unless that channel is live, rights-cleared, and specifically listed in the offer.

If additional channels are added later, list each actual channel and describe it accurately. Do not guarantee that a third-party publisher, search engine, or news platform will carry, index, retain, display, or rank an item. The Sector Journal can control only its own pages and any distribution it directly operates; it cannot promise removal or correction on an independent third-party site. A customer's purchase must not be described as Google News inclusion or guaranteed audience reach.

## 6. Fees, renewal, cancellation, and refunds — to decide

Before opening checkout, specify how payment is collected, when a subscription renews, how to stop renewal, whether cancellation stops future charges or also ends access, how unused credits are treated, whether any refund is available after work begins, how taxes and currency conversion apply, and who handles charge disputes. These rules must match the selected payment provider and applicable consumer/business law. Do not infer The Sector Journal's rules from another publisher's plans or terms.

## 7. Removal requests and external copies

Set out where customers may request a correction, update, or removal and how The Sector Journal assesses each request. Explain that The Sector Journal can act on its own site and directly controlled channels but cannot guarantee the removal of copies, caches, feeds, or reposts held by independent third parties. Preserve records needed to handle complaints, rights disputes, and legal requests under a documented retention policy.

## 8. Customer account, privacy, and security — to implement

Before adding a dashboard, document account security, user roles, organisation separation, password recovery, account closure, data retention, content backup, breach handling, support, and the payment and hosting providers involved. Update the privacy notice to match the real system. Do not collect customer uploads or payment details until this workflow and notice are in place.

## 9. Legal terms — counsel to complete

The final agreement must identify the legal service provider and its contact/registered details, applicable law and venue, intellectual-property licence for publication and agreed distribution, liability and remedies, complaints route, contract variation process, and any other terms required by the operating jurisdictions. Do not copy another publisher's clauses or jurisdiction without legal review.

## Reference benchmark reviewed

Industry Today's public materials describe its own package credits, customer accounts, directory listings, customer newsrooms, industry-specific sections, and distribution through partners, RSS, email, and social channels. Its pricing page shows NewsNow, Bing, Google News, and Yahoo examples, while its terms disclaim specific third-party placement/use and limit its ability to remove material outside its own network. Its pricing page describes some credit periods as 12 months, while its Enterprise sign-up page says selected credits are usable within 30 days; this is a reason to make The Sector Journal's validity and rollover rules consistent and explicit.
