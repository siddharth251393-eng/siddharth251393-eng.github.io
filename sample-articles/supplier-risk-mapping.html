A practical starting point for mapping supplier risk

Editorial demonstration — not published news.

This sample is provided to show IndustryWire’s intended explanatory style. It is based on the public references listed below but has not completed independent editorial verification. It does not describe a current company event, proprietary research result or market forecast.

Sample prepared 16 November 2026 · No author byline assigned

Supplier risk is often treated as a spreadsheet exercise: list vendors, assign a score and review the highest numbers. That can be a useful start, but it does not explain which impacts matter, how a company should respond, or whether its actions are changing outcomes.

The OECD’s responsible business conduct guidance offers a broader due diligence cycle. It describes embedding responsible business conduct in policies and management systems; identifying and assessing actual or potential impacts; ceasing, preventing or mitigating them; tracking implementation; communicating how impacts are addressed; and enabling remediation where appropriate. The process is intended to be risk-based and applicable across sectors and geographies.

Move from a vendor list to an impact map

A manufacturer can begin with the products and processes most critical to continuity, then trace the materials, services and business relationships behind them. The purpose is not to claim perfect visibility across every tier. It is to make gaps visible, decide where deeper assessment is warranted, and record the reasons for prioritisation.

Useful questions include: which inputs have few practical substitutes; where would a disruption affect safety or customer commitments; what evidence supports a supplier’s assurance; and which risks require action beyond a contract clause? Separate business continuity concerns from human-rights and environmental impacts, even when they overlap.

Make the process reviewable

Assign an accountable owner, document the criteria used to prioritise suppliers, set actions with dates, and revisit the assessment when operations or sourcing change. Track whether the action was completed and whether the underlying risk changed. A supplier score alone cannot answer that question.
